Since this is my first post on this blog, I guess this is my "Hello World." I really don't know where my writing will take me in due time, but I hope that this will help me process my thoughts and also serve as a time capsule I can reflect on. 

I'm thinking of separating the blog into two sections: "Essays" and "Notes." Essays will (hopefully) be more polished works on specific topics I find interesting or exciting (expect essays on tech and policy). Notes will just be random thoughts I want to put down from day-to-day.

I'm going to commit to not using any AI tools in writing this. While I think AI is hugely beneficial in tech and almost all aspects of STEM (I functionally use Claude Code for everything), I think there are few things more antithetical to our humanity and existence than to substitute self-expression with AI. 

Still, I'm not totally sure why I've felt so compelled to start this blog (and furthermore, why I'm choosing not to post it on Substack, etc). I don't feel concerned at all about reach with this, which feels like a nice reprieve from my social media presence. Maybe if I have an essay I'm really proud of, it might one day go on Substack or one of those platforms, but otherwise, I'm happy to confine my writing to this hidden subpage of my personal website. For a long time, I've had a desire to have full agency over my own thoughts and to find self-expression in new ways (even though I'm simultaneously not extroverted at all?), and I think that has manifest into pouring lots of time writing personal things without an audience, as well as my builds account with 25K+ followers. So, I suspect that I want this blog as an in-between? I think the fact that I'm meta-analyzing my own motivation for this blog is probably a sign that it's more of an unconcious decision, somehow motivated by the person I am (the "Elephant" of the Elephant and Rider metaphor introduced in "The Happiness Hypothesis," Jonathan Haidt).

Some of the biggest names in AI have incredibly insightful blogs of their own (Sam Altman, Dario Amodei, etc). I'm sure they are a source of influence, but I think it's exciting to go back to their earliest blog posts and see them discussing more innocuous things like the Affordable Care Act (Sam Altman). While I'm here, let me also credit Rithwik (Donner represent) with showing me his own blog and encouraging me to take the leap.

The last note I'll make about starting this blog is the comments system. Unfortunately, I really don't feel like setting up an entire backend server just to host comments for this tiny blog, so I'm using Giscus (which relies on Github Discussions, of all things) as a free way to host comments on a static site. This means that anyone who wants to comment will need a GitHub account, which sucks, but it's the best solution I've found. 

Regardless, I believe that starting a blog and writing in a way where you hit the "delete" button as few times as possible is one of the best ways to slow down and contemplate things (Aristotle would say that contemplation is the __divine__ life).
